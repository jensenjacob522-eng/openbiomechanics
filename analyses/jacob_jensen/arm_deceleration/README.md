Arm Injury Risk Is Driven by Angular Deceleration, Not Peak Joint Load
Core Thesis

Arm injury risk is driven by rate-dependent internal tissue loading during angular deceleration, rather than by peak joint torque or external load magnitude at layback.

Why Peak Metrics Miss Injury Risk

Peak values ignore rate-of-change effects

Passive tissues are viscoelastic and rate-sensitive

Injuries commonly occur during braking, not propulsion

Arm Deceleration Focus

This analysis focuses on the post–peak angular velocity (ball release → maximum internal rotation) phase of:

Shoulder internal rotation

Key Metrics

Peak angular velocity

Peak angular deceleration (most negative)
