# Arm Injury Risk Is Driven by Angular Deceleration, Not Peak Joint Load

## Core Thesis

Arm injury risk is driven by **rate-dependent internal tissue loading during angular deceleration**, rather than by peak joint torque or external load magnitude at layback.

Traditional injury metrics emphasize peak values (e.g., peak joint torque, peak angular velocity, or ball weight at maximal external rotation). However, these measures fail to capture the **temporal and rate-sensitive demands placed on passive and active tissues during the arm deceleration phase**, when injury commonly occurs.

---

## Why Peak Metrics Miss Injury Risk

- Peak values ignore **rate-of-change effects**
- Passive tissues (capsule, labrum, tendons) are **viscoelastic and rate-sensitive**
- Many throwing-related injuries occur during **braking**, not propulsion
- Similar peak velocities can produce **very different deceleration demands**

---

## Arm Deceleration Focus

This analysis focuses on the **post–peak angular velocity phase**  
(**Ball Release → Maximum Internal Rotation**) of:

- **Shoulder internal rotation**

This phase represents the primary braking window in the pitching motion, where large angular decelerations and impulses are required to dissipate rotational energy.

---

## Key Metrics

The following deceleration-specific metrics are computed for each pitch:

- **Peak angular velocity** (rad/s)
- **Peak angular deceleration** (most negative value, rad/s²)
- **Deceleration duration**  
  Time from peak velocity to 10% of peak (s)
- **Deceleration impulse**  
  \(\int | \alpha | \, dt\)

These metrics explicitly capture **rate-dependent internal loading**, rather than relying on single peak values.

---

## Dataset

- **Driveline OpenBiomechanics**
- Pitching motion capture data
- Joint kinematics sampled across the full throwing cycle

---

## Summary

This analysis demonstrates that:

- Peak angular velocity alone does **not** explain braking demand
- Peak joint loading at layback is insufficient to characterize injury risk
- **Angular deceleration magnitude, duration, and impulse** provide a more biomechanically meaningful representation of internal tissue stress during throwing

---

## Disclaimer

This work analyzes biomechanical loading characteristics and does **not** diagnose injury or establish causation.  
Results should be interpreted within the context of tissue tolerance, exposure, and individual adaptation.
